# SEO in 2026 — what actually changed, and what keel should ship

*Researched 2026-09-07. Sources at the end. **Re-verify before acting**: this area moved more in
eighteen months than in the previous ten years, and a stale SEO memo is worse than none.*

## The shift, stated plainly

The win condition moved from **rank the link** to **be the cited answer**. Google's AI Mode passed
**one billion monthly users** and is no longer experimental; queries have roughly doubled each
quarter since launch, and I/O 2026 redesigned the search bar itself — for the first time in 25 years
— for longer, conversational, multimodal input.

For a SaaS starter this matters in a narrow, concrete way: **the pages a buyer's marketing site
serves are now read by extractors as often as by rankers**, and the same markup serves both.

## What is settled

**1 · JSON-LD is the format, and it now has two audiences.** Preferred by Google, and the thing every
major AI system parses. Google's own framing is that *the structured data implemented for organic
search is the same markup Gemini reads* — so this is one implementation, not two.

**2 · HowTo is deprecated; FAQ is restricted — and the difference matters.**

**Corrected 2026-09-07 against the primary source.** An earlier draft of this memo, sourced from
agency blogs, asserted that "FAQ rich results were removed on 7 May 2026." Google's own Search
Central blog says something different, and the difference is not cosmetic:

> *HowTo* rich results are **deprecated** — no longer shown on desktop or mobile, and the
> documentation was removed. *FAQ* rich results **"will only be shown for well-known, authoritative
> government and health websites"** — restricted, not removed.

Google also states plainly: *"there's no need to proactively remove it. Structured data that's not
being used does not cause problems for Search, but also has no visible effects in Google Search."*

**The specific date "7 May 2026" could not be verified against any primary source and is not
repeated here.** The substantive change is documented by Google in 2023.

For a SaaS — not a government or health site — the practical conclusion is unchanged and now
properly grounded: **emit `FAQPage`, expect no rich result.** It is worth emitting because it
packages content as question–answer pairs, which is the shape an LLM quotes.

**3 · AI crawlers read JSON-LD with high confidence.** `PerplexityBot`, `ChatGPT-User` and peers
extract typed facts — organisation, product, pricing, location — rather than guessing from prose.
Correct markup is the difference between being quoted accurately and being paraphrased wrongly.

**4 · Structured data is correctness hygiene, not a ranking lever.** It makes a page legible. It does
not make it rank. Any guide promising otherwise is selling something, and a starter that implies it
would be making the marketing-claim the honesty rules forbid.

## What is contested, and must be shipped as such

**`llms.txt`** is an emerging convention for telling AI systems what a site is about. Adoption
reports are mixed and it is **not a confirmed Google ranking or retrieval signal**. keel should be
able to emit one and should say exactly that in the docs — shipping it silently as though it were
established would be the same overclaim.

## What keel ships, and what it deliberately does not

**Ships** — because these are primitives every SaaS marketing surface needs and getting them wrong is
invisible until traffic is missing:

| Primitive | Why it is not optional |
|---|---|
| Typed **JSON-LD builders** — `Organization`, `WebSite`, `SoftwareApplication`, `BreadcrumbList`, `FAQPage` | The five types a SaaS actually uses. Typed, so an invalid graph is a compile error rather than a Search Console warning three weeks later |
| **Canonical URLs** | Duplicate-content protection, and the single most common self-inflicted SEO wound |
| **`hreflang`**, per locale | keel ships i18n, which makes this **mandatory rather than nice** — an i18n site without `hreflang` competes with itself |
| **Open Graph + Twitter cards** | Every share is a rendering of your page you do not control otherwise |
| **Generated sitemap + robots** | Generated from the route tree, so it cannot drift from reality |
| **An explicit AI-crawler policy** | A *decision*, defaulted to allow — you want to be cited — with one documented place to refuse |
| **A performance budget that fails the build** | Core Web Vitals are measurable; a Lighthouse screenshot in a PR is not a budget |

**Does not ship** — a rank tracker, a keyword tool, a content generator, or any claim that a
primitive improves rankings. keel makes a page **correctly described and fast**. What is written on
it is the buyer's job, and no library changes that.

## The honesty line, and it is the same one everywhere else in keel

The temptation in this area is to imply causation: *"ship keel and rank higher."* That is
unmeasurable and therefore unsayable. What is true and sufficient: **keel's pages are described
correctly enough for a machine to quote them accurately, and fast enough not to be penalised.**
